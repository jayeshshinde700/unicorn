Festivals

Celebrating India's Festive Season

India celebrates throughout the year, and festivals are often the moments families most want to be together. For some, that now means a few days away from home.

30 Oct 20262 min readThe Unicon Journal
Share WhatsApp Facebook

A calendar that never really stops

Across the year there are festivals of light, colour, harvest, music and devotion, among them Diwali, Holi, Navratri and Durga Puja, Ganesh Chaturthi, Onam, Eid and Christmas. Customs and dates differ by region and by community, and many follow lunar calendars, so the exact days change every year. Always confirm dates for the year you plan to travel.

Why celebrate away from home?

Hosting a festival is joyful, but it is also a great deal of work. A stay at a hotel can let a family spend the occasion together without anyone being stuck in the kitchen or cleaning up afterwards. It also suits families whose relatives live in different cities and want a neutral meeting point.

The best festive trips are not about doing more. They are about being together with fewer chores.

Plan earlier than usual

Festive periods and the long weekends around them tend to be busy for travel. Trains, flights and hotel rooms are in demand, so decide your dates, confirm your group size and book as early as you can. If you are travelling with elders or young children, ask about room types, floor preferences and meal timings at the time of booking.

Make room for tradition

  • Carry small items that matter to you, such as a favourite diya, a family photograph or festive clothes.
  • Check with the hotel in advance about what is permitted in rooms. Open flames and incense are often restricted for safety reasons.
  • Ask whether special festive menus or events are planned, and whether dietary needs can be met.
  • Allow time for travel, as roads can be busier than usual around festive days.

Respect the occasion and the people around you

Festivals are shared spaces. Keep noise reasonable late at night, be mindful of other guests, and remember that a hotel hosts people of many traditions at once. Done thoughtfully, a festive stay can be warm, relaxed and memorable for everyone.
